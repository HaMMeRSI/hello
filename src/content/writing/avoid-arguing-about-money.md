We've all been there. A new month starts and you have no idea what the heck is going on with your money. You ask yourselves: Have we over-spent? Over-saved? Are we on track for that vacation we've been saving for? That unexpected expense definitely did not help.

You are filled with anxiety and dread. The plan is not working - that is if you even have a plan to begin with.

Then the arguing starts: "What is that expense?", "We said no shopping this month", "Why was the restaurant so expensive?" "From what should we cut next month?" "How should we improve?"

And this loop goes on and on, month after month.

You want to solve it, and already tried it all.
You've tracked your expenses for a week in that fancy spreadsheet but dropped it because it's too much of a hassle.
You tried 5 subscription apps but none was good enough for your needs, so you just told yourself that you won't go to that restaurant this month and pray - maybe it works for a month or two.

But, did you ever stopped for a second and wondered, why? "Why I feel lack of control?", "why he can, but I can't?"

Well, you can. This is solvable! With the correct mindset and a simple process. Let me show you how my wife and I did exactly that so you can too.

## My failure
"Save as much as possible" everyone told me. How hard could it be? I tried all the regular stuff: Looking at my bank account, spreadsheets, the apps - nothing worked. I couldn't be consistent with any of the solutions. There was something missing.

The bank account doesn't give me enough information, so I tried spreadsheet templates, but I just couldn't find 20 minutes every day to go over my daily expenses and filling in that abomination of a sheet. Apps did not improve on it either, same issue different interface. Filling in the name, date, category, price, currency, payment method and 11 other fields just to register that damn coffee in the morning? Hell no. So I gave up and did my best every month to save and "to hell with the consequences, it will be ok". First attempt to solve a problem failed.

I was younger and carefree, but with time I had more responsibilities, expenses grew, and I noticed that my savings got stuck. I couldn't tell why, but It bothered me, so I decided to try again.

The landscape didn't change much, same apps, same sheets - but one thing did change: ChatGPT 3.5 was introduced. I knew my biggest issue at the time was filling in forms, so I thought "why not let ChatGPT do the conversion for me?" I could tell it: "coffee 3$" and it would automatically fill those 17 fields. There wasn't anything like it, so I decided to build it myself. I'm a software engineer so I knew I could do it, **{{app}}**. Instead of filling 17 fields manually I needed to fix 1 or 2 every now and then. I added some nice analytics and called it done. I used it for a couple of months, but eventually I dropped it too. Once again my laziness got the better of me.

More time passed and the realization struck me again, that I was not progressing the way I wanted, so I had to stop and reflect - something was obviously wrong, and I needed to figure out what. It was the 3rd attempt and I already had gathered enough failures. I needed a method, a system that will solve my problem once and for all. AI is here, surely it can help me, no?

## Make AI your friend, not your enemy
Before I continue with the method that solved the issue, you need to understand that AI is a double edge sword - it either consumes you, or it will be your greatest ally.

Have you ever met a "meat-proxy"? The chad that passes Claudes answer unedited? - Yea, you do not want to be like him. He forgot how to think.

To understand how to use AI the right way, let us first understand how we solve problems in life: Imagine your brain as a fortress. Your job is to build it, one path, one room at a time, and each room has a label on it - the problem it solves. Inside the room, there are the skills, the methods and the tools you worked hard on to solve the problem. Some rooms are clean and tidy - those are your habits, job, hobbies, etc.
Others are messy and hard to get by. E.g. your personal finance room. You are struggling to solve these rooms problems because you lack the knowledge and tools to handle the problem.

The goal is to build more rooms, clean them, refine them, and there is only one known way to do that - thinking for yourself. If you let AI do the thinking for you, you stop building new rooms, but enter the same room, the "AI does it all" room. If you are not careful, your brain will slowly atrophy. The rooms you have already built will get messy, the paths will get blocked and eventually you forget they exist. In other words, you will lose important hard earned skills, and have trouble making decisions on your own. Hence you must surround the fortress with a lake infested with hungry alligators. You are the only one controlling who can access and how.

To use AI intelligently, we can think of it as the villagers surrounding our fortress.
There are good villagers and there are bad. It is up to you to call the right one into the fortress.

The bad ones are the ass-licking ones, those who are eager to "help" you by doing the thinking for you. They will hand you a solution to the room you are trying to build on a silver platter. Do not be tempted by them - this is the highway to atrophy world, because this is how you enter the wrong room without even noticing.
Do not take the solution, it is not yours!

And then there are the good villagers. You call a teacher when you don't know something, like teaching you about budgeting methods. Call a builder so it will create a tool that is part of the solution, like asking it to build a personal budget visualization. Ask a farmer to produce resources for the tool to use, like categorizing expenses per budget bucket. There are more villagers, as many as you need in fact. Make sure you call the right one.

Don't be a "meat-proxy" - think for yourself. Thats your job.

## Stop. Disconnect. Materialize. Do
The first step in order to take back control over your personal finances is to **stop** - accept the failure and understand you need to learn from it, and we learn by asking the right questions.

You sit down at the desk in your living room, and start doing the research. Suddenly you look around and notice the pile of dirty dishes - the association is instant! You need to clean it, because cleaning is the most important thing now. Ok, dishes are done, 30 minutes passed but you still have time, so you continue doing the work, but you get bored. Poof, without you even realizing, you have been holding your phone for over an hour watching TikToks.

For that exact reason I find it best to **disconnect** and change my environment. It doesn't need to be extreme (although it very much may help). I love working in the living room, since I find it spacious and cosy, but it is full of distractions. So I keep my phone away, sit with my back to the kitchen, and no music. Just me and the work that needs to be done. The living room is great for work I already started - for work I already know how to do, and have gotten used to. For something I find hard to even start I have the sea, and for deep work, fresh mind and new angles, the coffee shop around the corner is a great place.

Make it as easy as possible to be focused, and as hard as possible to get distracted.

Once you find yourself in the correct environment, start building the room.
Ask questions that will fill your room with new tools and knowledge.
E.g. "What budgeting systems are there?", "What is important to me to know month over month?"

Ask questions that will teach you how to use the new tools.
E.g. "How do I utilize the 50/30/20 budgeting system?", "Trends over time are cool, what trends can I create? What can I learn from them?"

After you gather some tools and knowledge, you'll find yourself asking "Why am I doing it this way?", "There must be a simpler way of doing that! How?" - These are the questions that will organize the room.
The more tools you know how to use efficiently, the easier it will get to solve the problem.

The most important thing: Do not keep your questions in your head, you need to **materialize** them. Write them down.

The room is messy? Choose the first tool you know to explore with.
The room is empty? Call a teacher, and ask it to give you the first tool or piece of knowledge to explore. Don't be tempted to answer those questions yet, let the flow continue, and don't interrupt yourself.
After you start asking the first questions you'll quickly notice two things:
1. New questions will follow - better questions.
2. You will get stuck
Once you get stuck - start writing the answers.
Do not know the answer? Make an educated guess, then call a teacher and ask it to correct and guide you.

Feel you've answered enough questions? **Do**. Fix the problem, then get feedback from your actions. You failed? Try again! Ask the teacher to explain a hard topic, ask a searcher to find you the right tool, or ask a builder to create the tool! Use it as much as possible, leverage it!
Got more questions because of the solution? Fantastic! Materialize, repeat!

I must urge you to remember not to call the bad villagers, because they are there, lurking. You must distinguish between what you are trying to solve and what you are not. Ask yourself "What room am I building?" - The personal finance room. Anything unrelated to it, AI can do for you. Anything related to it - AI must **not** do for you. Building an app, creating a spreadsheet, generating any tool are not part of the room. Understanding what budget system will work for you, understanding responsibilities, targets, and limits are part of the room.

## So, what changed?
I literally picked up a pen and a piece of paper, and started asking questions. I was brain dumping, asking and writing down anything that came to mind.
- How much have I saved so far?
- How much are we spending on any category actually?
- Whats my average monthly savings?
- What am I saving for? A vacation? A new car? A home?
- Do I have a goal?

While I was answering the questions, a specific one made me pause.
"Do I have a goal?" - umm no?
Poof - the room suddenly lit up, and for the first time I felt like I touched the real problem. It was never my laziness that made me stop. I was missing one simple thing to keep me going: a real goal. To some of you it might sound so trivial, but for me it was eye opening. My goal was basically: save as much money as possible. This is not a goal. This is an unmeasurable hope, and any distraction threw me off-course.

Once I knew the real problem, the solution came with it - a graph with 2 lines: one line represents the accumulated savings (income minus expenses) over time. The second line is the on track line - that is where our savings should roughly be each month, so I asked a builder to just add it to {{me\_app}}.

For our first goal my wife and I decided to start simple: our wedding celebration (We were already technically married before the party). We had a date, a rough estimate and our incomes.

By that time AI had become significantly better, so much so that it allowed us to change the entire process. Instead of recording every freaking expense, we would manage the monthly statements.

So we created a ritual.

On the second day of every month, we block 15 minutes in the evening and sit together. Then we download all of our transaction statements, credit-card, bank, etc. Excel, pdfs, text - anything goes, and we upload those to the app and voila - Everything is recorded with all the fields.
Next, there is only one thing we do: We look at that graph, and ask ourselves are we above or below the on track line?
Above it? Perfect. Keep at it!
Below it? Ok - Why? We go deeper.
We look at the categories, which is an outlier?
Anything unusual?
By how much did we overspend?
We gather information, see where we stand, and answer all of those annoying questions between us to avoid the argument all together.
The goal for the new month is to make up the deficit as well as our standard target.

We know there are tools today that allow us to skip the "download then upload" part. But my wife and I deliberately kept small friction. First, it demands our attention. Second, we find that by putting a little bit of effort the goal of the month sticks harder.

Minimum friction, maximum attention.

We have been doing this for almost a year now, and let me tell you this method works - at least for us. When we "failed" for two months straight, in the third, we closed the deficit. {{CTA}}

When editing this essay, I used a teacher to corroborate what we "guessed": the ritual and the goal. Just like the method I introduced. The teacher pointed me to a study by Harkin and his colleagues. In 2016 they pooled 138 experiments, almost 20,000 people, to check whether tracking your progress toward a goal actually helps. It does. It helps more when the information is physically recorded, and more when the outcome is reported to someone else.

I already hear you screaming at me: "But you are a software engineer! Of course you could build something to solve the problem!".
And I'll answer back: "My friend, today, you can too."

## The glass ceiling. Oh the ceiling...
Up until a year ago, software was reserved for the engineers who spent years refining the craft. Learning the language, the patterns, the process, one building block at a time. This is hard, and its not for nothing that software engineers are being paid well. But let me share with you a secret: as a software engineer with ten years+ of experience, I can tell you, no, I guarantee you, that the barrier to enter the app creation game has been completely and utterly destroyed - by AI.

Have you ever wished that a certain app existed? "Damn, I wish there was an app for... it would be worth millions". You searched, found nothing, and settled on that excel sheet, or power point. Depends on the problem.

There are three types of problems: core problems, common problems, and personal problems.

Core problems are problems that every problem can be broken into:
Data problem: excel
Presentation problem: power point
Communication problem: email
Etc. You get the gist.

You can combine several core solutions into a process to solve any kind of problem. The issue is that it sucks.
"When the only tool you have is a hammer - everything is a nail."

No thank you. This is where the apps for the common problems come into play. Someone did create a process out of those tools, automated it, and optimized it for the users. They turned several problems into a specialized problem. You can track your gym sessions and calorie intake in a word document ofc, but I bet you'd rather use an app.
Don't like the way that something is displayed? You think this element should be more prominent? Another element you've never used but is stuck right in your face? You'd completely remake that shitty app? Well, tough luck, you are not a programmer.

The last category of problems, the personal problems, do not have apps. I call those: "Hyper specialized apps". There is no money in them. A company that builds software would rather have a million paying users and be scored 6/10 than have 10 users and be scored 10/10.

No one will solve your problem but you. **You** need to create that hyper specialized app, see the vision and create the solution.

For years you thought that in order to create an app you need to know how to program, so your mind created a concrete ceiling between you and that app. But today, with the help of AI, this ceiling turned into glass. Shatter it.

After you are done with the thought process, after you have a clear problem, and a potential solution in mind - hand the builder your problem.
Ask it to build the solution.
Ask it to launch it.
Ask it to refine the design.
Ask it to fix the issue.
Ask it to create a new feature.
Ask it to move things around.
Ask it to remove stuff.

Ask it!

Think of the builder as the programmer, and you are the manager. Remember the room you are building - the personal finance room. Not the software engineering room.

You should not care about the code - how the thing actually works behind the scenes. All you should care about is **that the thing works**, and works for you, for your problem, not mine, not your friends, not that stranger. You.

## Nothing fits you, but you
Be the thinker, be the manager, call the teacher to help you, then call the builder and let it build your personal ideal solution. Guide it, and hold its hand.

Do yourself the courtesy of solving this once and for all. Bring your partner along on the journey, and avoid the next argument. You know it is coming if you don't change anything.

Shatter your glass ceiling - mastering AI **properly** is in and of itself a room you need to build. Once you master it, your journey of building more rooms will accelerate tenfold. Extend this skill to other areas of your life. Build the app you wish existed, automate that annoying process in your job. Build your friends wish - be the cool guy, be the star.
